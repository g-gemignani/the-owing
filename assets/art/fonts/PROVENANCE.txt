Taken from the STATIC builds. Google Fonts ships Cinzel only as a variable
`Cinzel[wght].ttf`; the static Bold comes from the upstream repository the Google
Fonts METADATA names as canonical, at the same 2.000 the variable is cut from.

Why these two.

Cinzel is Roman inscriptional — Trajan's lettering, which is what the frame kit is
already pretending to be: carved stone with a parchment inlay. It is the one display
face on the shortlist that agrees with the art direction instead of merely not
fighting it. BOLD rather than Regular because the display face is not only a title
face here: it is the card NAME, and `UI.fit_label` will take a name down to 7px in a
crowded fan. Cinzel Regular's hairline serifs are gone by then; Bold survives, and at
22px it reads as an inscription rather than as a shout.

Fira Sans was drawn for small text on screens, and it measured that way. Rendered at
12px against the shortlist (Alegreya Sans Regular and Medium, Spectral, and Godot's
own default) it has visibly the largest x-height and the most open counters of the
four, which is the whole requirement: card rules text shrinks to fit and 12px is a
normal outcome, not an edge case, so the body face has to be chosen at the size it
will actually be read at rather than at the size it looks handsome at.

Replacing either is a file swap: keep the names, drop a .ttf in this directory, run
`godot --headless --import`. `UITheme.display_face()` and `UITheme.body_face()` are
"use it if it exists", so deleting one falls back to the engine default rather than
to a blank screen — and the layout assertions in `tests/test_layout.gd` and
`tests/tooltip_test.gd` measure real wrapped line counts, so a face with much wider
metrics will say so.
