Music: generated for this project, not a downloaded pack.

Source: tools/gen_music.py (run under `nix shell nixpkgs#ffmpeg-headless`).
Licence: same as the rest of this repository — see ../../../LICENSE.

Why generated. No CC0 pack of *looping* game music turned up whose licence could be
verified the way the pixel packs' were, and picking tracks by ear is not a judgement
I can make honestly. Synthesising them is the same answer D29 reached for the
meaning-carrying UI symbols: author it, then measure it.

The sound effects were a downloaded set when this was written, and are not any more
(D150, ../PROVENANCE.txt): three CC0 Kenney packs at three sample rates over one
generated score is what "the sound is not uniform" sounded like. They are now built
from the same voices, at the same rate, in the same key as these tracks — which is
why `tools/gen_sfx.py` imports its tuning from `tools/gen_music.py` rather than
keeping a second copy of it.

Measurements for the shipped files are in measurements.json, regenerated on every
run of the script, which fails rather than writing a track that:

  * steps further across the loop point than the waveform steps anywhere else
    (an audible click, once per loop, forever),
  * peaks above 0.90 or sits outside an RMS band — music has to lose to the
    sound effects,
  * measures identically to another track, which would mean one loop shipped
    under several names.

Replacing any of these with real music is a file swap: keep the names in
`Audio.SCORES` and drop OGG files in this directory.
