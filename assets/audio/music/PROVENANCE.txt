Music: generated for this project, not a downloaded pack.

Source: tools/gen_music.py, built on tools/audio_voices.py — the instrument the sound
        effects are also built from. Run under `nix shell nixpkgs#ffmpeg-headless`.
Licence: same as the rest of this repository — see ../../../LICENSE.
Format:  5 looping tracks, OGG Vorbis, mono, 44.1 kHz, 31-48 s each.

Why generated. No CC0 pack of *looping* game music turned up whose licence could be
verified the way the pixel packs' were, and picking tracks by ear is not a judgement I can
make honestly. Synthesising them is the same answer D29 reached for the meaning-carrying UI
symbols: author it, then measure it.

Rewritten in D173. The first five tracks (D33) were square-wave arpeggios and sine pads over
a four-chord progression, dry, at 22.05 kHz — and every one of them had a beat. Measured
with the pulse metric added for this pass, the old set scores 0.48 (menu), 0.23 (overworld),
0.59 (dungeon), 0.78 (combat) and 0.58 (boss). A dungeon you can tap along to is a dungeon
you stop being afraid of.

What replaced them is a place rather than a tune:

  * drones and modal colour instead of a progression — a progression says where the music
    is going, a drone says where you are,
  * no pulse at all outside a fight. The town, the overworld and the dungeon have phrases
    at intervals nobody can count; the drums arriving MEAN a fight,
  * a stone room on every track (a Schroeder reverb, primed with the loop's own tail so the
    seam stays exact),
  * modelled instruments: a plucked string, low bowed strings, a far-off formant choir,
    struck metal, frame drums and moving air.

Nothing here is transcribed from anything. Drones, minor seconds, tritones and bare fifths
are the shared vocabulary of dark fantasy the way a minor key is the vocabulary of sad; the
phrases, the instrument set and the arrangements are this project's.

The sound effects were a downloaded set when this file was first written, and are not any
more (D150, ../PROVENANCE.txt). They are built from the same voices, at the same rate, in
the same key as these tracks — which is why both generators import `tools/audio_voices.py`
rather than keeping a second copy of the instrument, and why `tools/gen_sfx.py` checks its
own register and rate against the measurements in THIS directory.

Measurements for the shipped files are in measurements.json, regenerated on every run of the
script, which fails rather than writing a track that:

  * steps further across the loop point than the waveform steps anywhere else (an audible
    click, once per loop, forever),
  * peaks above 0.90 or sits outside an RMS band — music has to lose to the sound effects,
  * measures brighter than the centroid ceiling, or lighter than the bass floor,
  * keeps time when it should not, or fails to when it should (the pulse gate above),
  * has a hole in it: the quietest two seconds must be at least 30% of the track's own
    average, because phrases are now up to 4.6 s apart and a gap with nothing under it
    reads as the music having stopped rather than as space,
  * measures identically to another track, which would mean one loop shipped under several
    names.

Replacing any of these with real music is a file swap: keep the names in `Audio.SCORES` and
drop OGG files in this directory.
