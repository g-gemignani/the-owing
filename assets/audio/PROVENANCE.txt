Sound effects: generated for this project, not a downloaded pack.

Source: tools/gen_sfx.py (run under `nix shell nixpkgs#ffmpeg-headless`).
Licence: same as the rest of this repository — see ../../LICENSE.

Why generated (D150). These were three CC0 Kenney packs, and the packs do not agree
with each other about what kind of game this is. The file headers were the receipt:
16 files at 48 kHz stereo (RPG Audio: recorded foley), 5 at 44.1 kHz mono (Interface
Sounds: soft modern UI blips), 5 at 44.1 kHz stereo (Music Jingles: 8-bit chiptune
fanfares) — over a score of our own at 22.05 kHz mono. So winning played a chiptune
fanfare, taking a hit played a foley recording, and clicking a button played a modern
blip. Every file was fine; the set was three sets.

Three packs cannot be reconciled by turning knobs, because the difference is the
instrument and not the level. The 23 effects here are synthesised from four voices —
square, triangle, filtered noise, a two-operator bell — at the score's sample rate,
in the score's key (A natural minor), on one stated loudness ladder: the interface
under the world, the world under the stingers, all of it over the music.

Measurements for the shipped files are in measurements.json, regenerated on every run
of the script, which fails rather than writing a set that:

  * puts a file outside its family's loudness, length or register band,
  * spans more than 22 dB of RMS end to end, which is not one set,
  * lets the interface family span more than x2.6 in timbre — menu sounds play
    back-to-back and mean nearly the same thing, so they have to BE one instrument,
  * ships two effects that measure identically under two names.

The three Kenney licence files that used to sit here are gone with the packs they
covered. Nothing in this directory came from outside the repository.

Replacing any of these with recorded or commissioned sound is a file swap: keep the
names in `Audio.SOUNDS` and drop OGG files here.
